Jasmine Banks

Media and social psychologist

My name is Jasmine Banks, I am a 6th-year Ph.D. Candidate in Psychology and Digital Studies at the University of Michigan. I am on the job market as of August 2025!

I am a multidisciplinary researcher thats interested in world building practices of humans, especially as they intersect with technology. My research spans both academic and applied contexts while also exploring public scholarship and artistic practices as ways of generating and sharing knowledge. I draw on qualitative, mixed-methods and experimental methods in my work. I also have experience conducting community based research, curriculum development and program evaluation working with homeless shelters, schools and other programs.

Alongside my scholarship, I conduct user research that draws on psychology to uncover not just what people do with technology, but why. This perspective allows me to translate user experiences into insights for design and strategy.

About

I graduated from Florida International University in 2019 with a Bachelor of Arts in Psychology, a minor in Sociology, and a certificate in Women and Gender Studies. My research career began as a research assistant at the Power, Women and Relationship's Lab under the guidance of Dr. Asia Eaton where I first gained experience in qualitative research working with women who have experienced homelessness.

Beyond this project, I also have experience with other methods such as photo-voice, focus groups, questionnaires, and intervention development. My current research focuses on understanding how digital technologies shape human relationships, identity formation, and community building, with particular attention to issues of equity, ethics, and social justice.

Research Areas

Exploring the intersection of technology, identity, and human connection

Digital Identity & Culture

Investigating how individuals construct and navigate identity in digital spaces, with particular focus on how social media platforms, algorithms, and online communities shape self-presentation and cultural expression.

Love in the Algorithm Age

Examining how algorithmic systems shape romantic connections and dating experiences, particularly focusing on the experiences of Black women and other marginalized groups in digital dating environments.

Dating Apps Digital Intimacy Algorithmic Matching Online Romance

Ethical AI & Human Connection
