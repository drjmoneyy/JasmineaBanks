Jasmine Banks

Media & Social Psychologist • World Builder • Storyteller

My name is Jasmine Banks, I am a 6th-year Ph.D. Candidate in Psychology and Digital Studies at the University of Michigan. I am on the job market as of August 2025!

I am a multidisciplinary researcher thats interested in world building practices of humans, especially as they intersect with technology. My research spans both academic and applied contexts while also exploring public scholarship and artistic practices as ways of generating and sharing knowledge. I draw on qualitative, mixed-methods and experimental methods in my work. I also have experience conducting community based research, curriculum development and program evaluation working with homeless shelters, schools and other programs.

Alongside my scholarship, I conduct user research that draws on psychology to uncover not just what people do with technology, but why. This perspective allows me to translate user experiences into insights for design and strategy.

About

My journey started at Florida International University, where I earned my BA in Psychology (2019) with a minor in Sociology and a certificate in Women and Gender Studies. But here's where it gets interesting—my first real research experience was working with Dr. Asia Eaton at the Power, Women and Relationship's Lab, diving deep into the stories of women experiencing homelessness.

That experience changed everything for me. It showed me the power of qualitative research to capture human complexity in ways that numbers alone never could. From there, I fell in love with methods that center people's voices—photo-voice projects, focus groups, community-based research, even program evaluation work with shelters and schools.

Now? I'm that researcher who gets genuinely excited about methodological creativity. Give me a research question about digital identity, and I'm already brainstorming whether we need interviews, surveys, digital ethnography, or maybe something totally new. I believe the best research happens when we meet people where they are—literally and metaphorically.

Digital Love & Connection

AI Ethics & Justice

Making sure our AI future doesn't suck for marginalized people. I investigate bias in algorithmic systems and advocate for AI that actually serves justice, equity, and human flourishing.

Identity & Intersectionality Online

How do you be Black, be a woman, be queer, be yourself online when platforms weren't designed with you in mind? I explore the creative ways marginalized folks navigate digital identity and build belonging.

Creative Research Methods

I'm a methodological maximalist—photo-voice, digital ethnography, community-based participatory research, you name it. If there's a creative way to center people's voices and experiences, I'm here for it.

Research Areas

Exploring the intersection of technology, identity, and human connection

Digital Identity & Culture

How do we become ourselves online? I'm obsessed with the intricate ways people craft identity across platforms—from carefully curated Instagram grids to vulnerable TikTok confessions. It's not just about self-presentation; it's about how algorithms, community norms, and platform affordances shape who we think we can be.

Social Media Digital Culture Identity Formation Self-Presentation

Love in the Algorithm Age

Swipe right for algorithmic bias? My research dives into how dating apps aren't just matching people—they're shaping who gets to be desirable, loveable, and seen. I'm particularly passionate about understanding how Black women navigate these digital dating landscapes and the ways algorithms can either perpetuate or challenge romantic hierarchies.

Dating Apps Digital Intimacy Algorithmic Matching Online Romance

Ethical AI & Human Connection

AI isn't neutral—it's made by humans, trained on human data, and deployed in human contexts. I'm committed to ensuring that as AI becomes more embedded in our social lives, it actually serves justice and equity rather than amplifying existing biases. Because if we're going to have robot overlords, they better be fair ones! 🤖

Past Research Projects

Completed research studies and investigations

Project YUNG

Completed

Ever wonder how Gen Z actually figures out who they are through their phones? Project YUNG was my deep dive into young adults' digital relationship landscapes—how they flirt on Instagram, find community on Discord, and navigate the messy intersection of algorithms and attraction.

Culture of Care

Completed

What makes some online spaces feel like home? This project explored the beautiful ways people create cultures of care in digital communities—from mental health support groups to fan communities that become chosen families. Turns out, good design can actually facilitate love.

Neighborhoods of Refuge

Completed

When mainstream platforms fail you, you build your own sanctuary. This study documented the brilliant ways marginalized communities carve out safe digital spaces and develop strategies to resist algorithmic discrimination. It's about digital activism, creative resistance, and the power of community.

Adolescent Girls' Perceptions

Completed

Teenage girls are the first generation to grow up with AI-powered social media algorithms. This research explored how these young women experience and interpret algorithmic curation of their social worlds—and what it means for their relationships with themselves and each other.
